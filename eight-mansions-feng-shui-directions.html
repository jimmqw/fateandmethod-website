Eight Mansions Feng Shui: Find Your Auspicious Directions

The Ba Zhai school reveals which directions heal, which ones energize, and which ones you should avoid. A complete guide to using your Kua number to unlock the directional power of your space.

Home / Feng Shui / Eight Mansions Directions

Feng shui is ultimately a practice of alignment -- positioning yourself, your belongings, and your activities in harmony with the energetic flow of your environment. The Eight Mansions (Ba Zhai) school takes this principle and makes it deeply personal: your Kua number, derived from your birth year, determines which directions are your allies and which are your adversaries. Align with your favorable directions and you work with the current; face your unfavorable directions and you swim against it, every day, without knowing why.

What Is the Eight Mansions School?

The Eight Mansions school, also called Ba Zhai, is one of the two major schools of feng shui practice (the other being the Flying Stars school). Where Flying Stars analyzes the changing energy of time and space across a 20-year cycle, Eight Mansions is primarily concerned with the fixed relationship between a person and their environment based on their birth chart. It divides all directions into two categories: East Group (East, Southeast, South, and North) and West Group (West, Northwest, Northeast, and Southwest).

Your group membership, combined with your specific Kua number within that group, determines your four favorable and four unfavorable directions. This is not a generalized recommendation -- it is a specific, calculable alignment that you can apply immediately to how you arrange your home and how you position yourself within it.

Key Concept: East vs. West Group

The East Group includes Kua numbers 1, 3, 4, and 9. The West Group includes Kua numbers 2, 5, 6, 7, and 8. The fundamental rule of Eight Mansions: East Group people thrive in East Group directions and should avoid West Group directions. West Group people thrive in West Group directions and should avoid East Group directions.

How to Calculate Your Kua Number

Your Kua number is calculated from your birth year. The formula differs slightly for men and women, and there is a crucial concept called the "nearest decade rule" that applies if your birth year falls between January 1st and February 4th -- you may need to use the previous year for the calculation.

  1. 1

    Take the last two digits of your birth year

    For someone born in 1985, you start with 85. For 2003, you start with 03.

  2. 2

    Add the two digits together

    85 becomes 8 + 5 = 13. Reduce again: 1 + 3 = 4.

  3. 3

    For men: subtract from 10

    10 - 4 = 6. This is your base Kua number.

  4. 4

    For women: add 5

    4 + 5 = 9. If the result is two digits, add them again: 9 is final.

Exception: If your result is 5, it does not appear as a final Kua number. For men, 5 becomes 2. For women, 5 becomes 8. These are the only two substitutions for 5.

The Four Favorable Directions

Once you have your Kua number, the four favorable directions are determined. Each has a specific quality and purpose -- they are not interchangeable. Use the right direction for the right activity.

Sheng Qi -- Best Direction

Vitality & Good Fortune

The foremost favorable direction -- the direction of overall prosperity, growth, and good energy. Face this direction when you want to feel energized, when starting something new, or when you need an overall boost to your fortune. It is the direction of upward momentum.

生气 -- The direction of life-generating energy

Tian Yi -- Heavenly Doctor

Health & Healing

The direction associated with well-being and recovery from illness. Face this direction when you are unwell, when visiting a doctor, when taking medication, or when you want to support long-term health maintenance. It is the direction of restoration.

天医 -- The direction of celestial healing

Yan Nian -- Harmony

Relationships & Longevity

The direction of human relationships and sustained harmony -- including marriage, family relationships, and professional partnerships. Face this direction to improve existing relationships or to attract harmonious new ones. Also associated with longevity in the classical texts.

延年 -- The direction of extended life and connection

Sheng Mu -- Mother

Protection & Support

The direction associated with nurturing, support, and safety. This is the direction to face when you need a sense of security -- during vulnerable periods, when facing challenges you are unsure about, or when you want to create a stable, supportive environment. It is the direction of groundedness.

生母 -- The direction of maternal protection

The Four Unfavorable Directions

Equally important as knowing where to face is knowing where to avoid. The four unfavorable directions create draining, disruptive, or harmful energy when faced over extended periods. This does not mean you should never face these directions -- it means you should not face them for sleep, desk work, or extended activities.

Jue Ming -- Loss of Life

Financial Loss & Misfortune

The most harmful of the unfavorable directions. Extended exposure can lead to financial loss, illness, and overall declining fortune. This is the direction to absolutely avoid for beds, desks, and primary seating.

绝命 -- The direction that severs fortune

Wu Gui -- Five Ghosts

Hidden Dangers & Deception

The direction associated with hidden threats, betrayal, and unexpected misfortune. It creates a sense of being watched or hunted without knowing why. This direction disrupts sleep and creates anxiety. It is particularly dangerous in relationships -- avoid for bedroom and shared spaces.

五鬼 -- Five ghosts create hidden mischief

Liu Sha -- Six Killings

Conflict & Resistance

The direction of friction and opposition. Facing this direction creates arguments, legal disputes, and chronic tension in relationships. It is a direction of fighting against the current -- nothing flows easily when you face Liu Sha.

六煞 -- Six kills create constant friction

Huo Hai -- Ho Rui --祸害

Obstacles & Disruption

The direction of persistent obstacles and hindrances. Facing this direction does not create immediate dramatic misfortune -- instead it creates a grinding sense that nothing is going right, that progress is blocked, and that efforts are not yielding results.

祸害 -- The direction of hidden obstruction

How to Apply This in Your Space

1. Your Desk and Workspace

Sit facing your Sheng Qi direction (best overall direction) or your Tian Yi direction (health direction) when working. This is the single highest-impact change you can make if you work from home. Use a compass app to verify orientation -- not Google Maps, which can be off by 5-15 degrees in urban areas. Stand at your desk, face the direction, and check that the front of your computer monitor is also roughly oriented in that direction.

2. Your Bed

The bed is the second most important placement. Sleep is when your body recovers and your spirit is most open -- facing an unfavorable direction overnight, every night, creates cumulative drain that manifests as poor sleep, persistent low-level anxiety, or chronic health issues that do not resolve. Face your favorable directions for sleep: Yan Nian or Sheng Mu are ideal for the bedroom, as they support relationships and restoration respectively.

3. Your Front Door

The direction your front door faces is a collective energy entry point for the household. If your front door faces an unfavorable direction, the remedies are more complex -- you may need a feng shui cure at the door, a feng shui mirror, or in some cases, it may indicate that the home's energy is fundamentally misaligned with the occupant. A professional consultation is advisable for front door issues, as they affect the entire household.

4. Your Stove (Kitchen)

The stove represents the hearth energy of the household -- the source of nourishment and financial prosperity. The stove should face your favorable direction, particularly Sheng Qi (best overall) or Tian Yi (health). This is a well-established principle in the classical Eight Mansions texts and one that can be adjusted with minimal disruption in most kitchen layouts.

Common Mistakes in Eight Mansions Practice

Ignoring the Near-Year Transition: If you were born between January 1st and February 4th, your Chinese zodiac year has not yet changed when the Gregorian calendar flips. A person born on January 25th, 1990 is still, for feng shui purposes, born in 1989. Use a Chinese zodiac calendar to confirm which year your birth falls in before calculating your Kua number.

Confusing Compass Degrees with Cardinal Directions: When we say "face South" in Eight Mansions practice, we mean facing within approximately 15-30 degrees of due South. You do not need to sit with your back perfectly to North. The energy is directional but not that precisely calibrated. Use a compass, but do not treat it as a laser -- there is room for adjustment based on what is practical in your space.

Applying It to Temporary Situations: Eight Mansions directions matter most for where you spend extended time -- your primary desk, your bed, your primary seating area. Facing an unfavorable direction in a hotel room for one night is not going to materially harm you. The concern is cumulative, habitual exposure over months and years.
